Evaluating & Deploying: The Interactive Workflows

Hope is not an infrastructure strategy; data-driven capacity planning is. Experience the workflows that make this possible.

To truly understand the value of the OpenShift AI Model Catalog, you must experience how it replaces trial-and-error with mathematical, governed workflows.

Below are four focused, interactive simulations. Step into the shoes of both the AI Engineer and the Platform Administrator to see how OpenShift AI secures the supply chain from discovery to deployment.

Workflow 1: The Showroom (Discovery & Filtering)

The Goal: Find a compliant, Red Hat Validated model while filtering out unsupported community assets. The Value: Mitigating "Shadow AI" by ensuring data scientists only use approved, secure foundation models.

Simulation 1: Filtering the Catalog

  • Click through the simulation below. * Notice how the left sidebar allows users to filter models by specific tasks, providers, and licenses.

  • Observe how Red Hat Validated models are clearly badged, separating them from untested community options.

Workflow 2: Data-Driven Provisioning (Performance Insights)

The Goal: Determine the exact hardware required to meet a specific Service Level Agreement (SLA). The Value: Preventing expensive over-provisioning and avoiding under-resourced pod crashes.

Simulation 2: Sizing the Hardware

  • Click through the simulation below.

  • Open the Performance Insights tab for a validated model.

  • Adjust the Max Latency and Min RPS sliders. Watch how hardware configurations (like the H200 or A100) that fail to meet these thresholds are instantly hidden, removing the guesswork from capacity planning.

Workflow 3: The Guardrailed Deployment

The Goal: Deploy the selected model to the cluster without causing an outage or selecting the wrong runtime. The Value: Protecting cluster uptime and enforcing infrastructure guardrails.

Simulation 3: Deploying the Model

  • Click through the simulation below.

  • Notice how the wizard automatically selects the best Serving Runtime based on the model framework.

  • Pay attention to the Deployment Strategy selection. You must explicitly choose between a Rolling update (zero downtime, requires overlapping GPU capacity) or Recreate (brief downtime, saves GPU resources).

Workflow 4: Enterprise Governance (New in 3.3)

The Goal: Take control of the catalog as a Platform Administrator. The Value: Enforcing strict corporate compliance and injecting proprietary assets.

Simulation 4: Catalog Management

  • Click through the simulation below.

  • Explore the new 3.3 Catalog Management UI.

  • See how administrators can quickly inject custom catalog sources to expose fine-tuned, internal models.

  • Observe the Allow/Disallow capabilities, allowing admins to selectively hide or remove specific models to comply with internal security or regulatory restrictions.

Next Steps

You have now experienced the UI-driven workflows for discovery, evaluation, deployment, and curation. For enterprise-scale environments, however, Platform Engineers often prefer to manage these configurations as code.
